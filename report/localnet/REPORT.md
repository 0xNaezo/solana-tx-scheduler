## 2. Ordering rule per validator (primary evidence: our transactions)

Unit: pairs of our transactions that landed in the same slot. **D** = share of *disagree* pairs (higher fee sent later) where the higher fee landed first: 1 = priority, 0 = arrival order (FIFO), 0.5 = neither. D counts pairs sent ≥5 ms apart; closer ones reach the leader in an order we do not control. **A** = share of *agree* pairs where the common order held: ≈0.5 means noise. τ = Kendall tau of landing order with priority / with send order (all same-slot pairs). 95% CIs bootstrap windows; p-values from 10000 within-slot permutations, Holm-adjusted over all 2 tests. Pre-registered rule: a label needs ≥30 disagree pairs from ≥5 windows; p_D < 0.05 then D ≥ 0.8 priority, D ≤ 0.2 FIFO, else mixed; otherwise p_A < 0.05 = mixed/balanced, else no rule. A rule that exists becomes "priority within T ms batches" when that model (§4) beats both FIFO and priority by ≥5 points of pair accuracy.

## 4. Model prediction vs. fact

Share of same-slot pairs predicted correctly by FIFO (send order), by Agave-style priority (reward/cost; our transactions have equal cost, so this is cu_price order), and by the best pairwise batching model "priority if sent within B ms of each other, send order beyond". Batch length T: maximum-likelihood fit of P(priority wins | gap g) = ε + (1 − 2ε)·max(0, 1 − g/T), ε = 0.05, over disagree pairs (fixed-length batches at an unknown phase; the best B sits near T/2). The design has no send gaps between ~0.2 and 10 ms, so a fit below 10 ms reads "≤10 ms". Exact-priority slots: slots with ≥3 of our transactions in exactly descending fee order.
